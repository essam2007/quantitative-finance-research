# MartingaleLab: A Test-Driven Monte Carlo Derivatives Pricing Library

## 1. The recommendation

Build **MartingaleLab**: one installable, pytest-validated Python package that prices European, path-dependent, and American options by Monte Carlo — where every probability theorem you rely on (the martingale property of discounted prices, quadratic variation and Ito's correction, the CLT behind Monte Carlo error bars, Girsanov's change of measure, optional stopping) is encoded as an automated test the library must pass. It wins for you specifically because your repo's gap is not topics — you have already touched Brownian motion, Monte Carlo with variance reduction, Heston, and an implied-vol surface — but depth: everything is a single-file `engine.py` with no tests, no shared structure, and no validation harness. This project introduces almost no new subject matter; instead it forces depth on exactly your three named goals: real software engineering becomes the spine (src layout, pytest, CI), the derivatives ladder follows the consensus curriculum taught at UvA, Columbia IEOR, Stevens FE621, and Baruch, and your README's measure-theoretic aspirations become falsifiable numerical experiments. All three judges on the review panel picked this proposal. One honesty adjustment up front: the original 12-week pitch was optimistic. Budget **16–20 part-time weeks** (6–10 hours/week alongside coursework); the roadmap below plans 18 weeks with a pre-agreed cut order so that stopping early still leaves a finished artifact.

## 2. Why this over the alternatives

Be aware of what the panel actually decided: all three finalist proposals were variants of the *same* architecture — a test-driven Monte Carlo pricing library ending in a real-data implied-vol exercise. The choice was between executions, not directions, and the winning plan below absorbs the best pieces of both runners-up.

- **MartLab (Proposal 2)** was a near-clone with slightly slimmer scope (smiles instead of a full surface, exotics compressed into one milestone) but weaker sequencing — it used importance sampling before teaching the Girsanov theory that explains it. Its best ideas are grafted into the roadmap: the self-financing accounting invariant in the hedging lab, foresight bias demonstrated as a failing test rather than a warning, the Yahoo-IV forensics exhibit, and the TestPyPI/mkdocs finishing moves. If your available time collapses, the cut order below effectively turns this project into MartLab — that is by design.
- **Martingale Lab (Proposal 3)** had the best pure-probability pedagogy (an enumerable binomial sample space where the martingale property is checked *exactly*, and a gambler's-ruin optional-stopping lab) and the most finishable scope, but it demoted Greeks, American Monte Carlo, barriers, and all real data to stretch goals. For someone who named derivatives as a co-equal goal, that is the wrong emphasis: Greeks are not optional for anyone claiming derivatives depth, and a report capstone is less motivating and less recruiter-visible than a real smile. Its two standout labs are grafted in below as Milestones 2 and 9a. Prefer its shape only if your goal drifts toward pure probability or math-grad-school preparation.

## 3. What you'll learn

### Derivatives
Risk-neutral valuation as a mechanism rather than a slogan: the binomial risk-neutral measure derived from no-arbitrage, pricing as a discounted Q-expectation, put-call parity as a model-free identity, Greeks and the three ways desks estimate them by simulation, early exercise as optimal stopping, and — the milestone where options finally "click" — delta hedging as replication, with gamma P&L equal to realized-minus-implied variance. The capstone confronts the market's correction to Black-Scholes: the volatility smile, and the discovery that implied vol is a model *output* driven by your rate and dividend inputs, not a market fact.

### Probability
Every abstraction in your README becomes a measurement: conditional expectation as a computable operator on an enumerated sample space; the martingale property of discounted prices holding under Q and demonstrably failing under P; quadratic variation converging to T (so (dW)² = dt is observed, not asserted); LLN, CLT, and the 1/√N error rate as the operational meaning of a Monte Carlo price; variance reduction as applied probability (antithetics = covariance, control variates = conditional expectation and regression, stratification = law of total variance); Girsanov's theorem as a pathwise reweighting you can run — and the same machinery reappearing as importance sampling; stopping times and the optional stopping theorem, the single most interview-tested martingale tool. These internals double directly as green-book quant-interview material.

### Coding craft
The jump from notebook scripts to a real library: packaging (pyproject.toml, src layout, editable installs), pytest with parametrized and property-based tests, GitHub Actions CI, seeded RNG discipline and dependency injection, interfaces/protocols and composition (payoffs, path generators, estimator decorators), statistical testing of stochastic code with principled tolerances, benchmark harnesses that produce reproducible tables, a real-data pipeline with caching and cleaning as first-class code, and a technical report that regenerates end-to-end from one command. You will also practice the hardest discipline: refactoring and *retiring* your own old code.

## 4. The roadmap

**Structure.** Phase A (weeks 1–9) is the mandatory core — stop there and you still own a coherent, tested library demonstrating martingale pricing, error-bar discipline, variance reduction, and Girsanov. Phase B (weeks 10–18) is the extension track, ordered by interview value. One flex module (barriers) sits outside the spine.

**Pre-agreed cut order if coursework crunch hits:** (1) drop the flex barrier module; (2) drop Longstaff-Schwartz but keep tree-based American pricing (Milestone 9 shrinks to one week); (3) scope the capstone down to a single expiry's smile on cached or synthetic data. **Never cut:** the hedging lab, the smile with your own solver, or the final report — they carry the most interview value. Do not add anything sideways (Heston calibration, ML, backtests) until the ladder is done; your repo already demonstrates the breadth failure mode.

**Standing rule from day one:** notebooks may only *call* `martingalelab`, never define pricing logic. This single rule is what prevents relapse into the `engine.py` pattern. Build the package inside your existing repo (e.g. `src/martingalelab/`), retiring each superseded build (`quant_builds/monte_carlo`, the standalone Brownian scripts) with a short pointer README — the git history itself becomes the "breadth refactored into depth" story.

### Phase A — the core (weeks 1–9)

**Milestone 1 — Package skeleton and the Black-Scholes oracle (Week 1)**
- *Build:* The `martingalelab` package: pyproject.toml, src layout, pinned dependencies, pytest, ruff, GitHub Actions. Copy the packaging/CI boilerplate from any minimal modern template rather than hand-writing it — the learning target is the structure and the tests, not TOML syntax. Then `analytic.py`: Black-Scholes(-Merton) price and all closed-form Greeks (delta, gamma, vega, theta, rho), typed, vectorized, rewritten from the d1/d2 derivation — not ported. Also write `docs/TESTING.md`, the project's test constitution (see Section 5); it is load-bearing, not optional.
- *Key concept:* Risk-neutral valuation; N(d1)/N(d2) as probabilities; put-call parity as a model-free arbitrage identity.
- *Validation:* Parity C − P = S − K·e^(−rT) to 1e-12 across a randomized parameter grid (catches sign and discounting bugs instantly); each analytic Greek matches a central finite difference of your own price to ~1e-6; price monotone increasing in vol; values match py_vollib to near machine precision and Hull's worked textbook examples (DerivaGem-checkable) kept as pytest fixtures. CI green on push.

**Milestone 2 — The binomial martingale world (Week 2)**
- *Build:* A discrete module: an N-step binomial model with a fully enumerable sample space (all 2^N coin-flip paths for small N), conditional expectation implemented as an operator on path functionals, the risk-neutral probability q = (e^(r·dt) − d)/(u − d) derived from no-arbitrage, and European pricing two ways — full expectation over the enumerated space, and backward induction. Keep the tree code; it returns in Milestone 9 for American exercise.
- *Key concept:* Filtrations, conditional expectation, and the martingale property made *exact* — machine-precision contact with measure-theoretic objects before any statistical tolerance exists; CRR-to-Black-Scholes convergence as a CLT in disguise.
- *Validation:* Enumeration price equals backward-induction price to machine precision; E_Q[e^(−r·dt) S_{n+1} | F_n] = S_n exactly at every node — plus the test asserting this *fails* under the real-world probability p; parity to 1e-12; convergence plot to Black-Scholes reproducing the characteristic odd/even-step oscillation.

**Milestone 3 — Path engine and the stochastic-calculus lab (Weeks 3–4)**
- *Build:* `engine/paths.py`: a PathGenerator abstraction (seeded `numpy.random.Generator` injected, never global state) producing Brownian and GBM paths via exact simulation, Euler-Maruyama, and Milstein behind one interface. Refactor your existing `brownian_motion.py` and `geometric_brownian_motion.py` to consume it and retire the originals — first proof the library subsumes old work. Add the Ito lab notebook: sum squared Brownian increments over refining partitions; show the naive chain rule fails on log(GBM) while the ½f″ correction closes the gap.
- *Key concept:* Quadratic variation — (dW)² = dt as an observable measurement; strong vs weak convergence of SDE schemes; discretization error as a bug class distinct from statistical error.
- *Validation:* Quadratic variation converges to T as partitions refine; increments have mean 0, variance dt, and are uncorrelated across disjoint intervals; identical seeds give bitwise-identical paths (reproducibility test); fitted log-log convergence slopes ~0.5 strong for Euler, ~1.0 for Milstein, per Higham; exact-GBM terminal distribution passes a KS test against the lognormal closed form.

**Milestone 4 — Monte Carlo pricing with error bars (Weeks 5–6)**
- *Build:* A Payoff protocol (call, put, digital) and an MCResult dataclass carrying estimate, standard error, 95% CI, path count, and runtime — the library never returns a bare point estimate. Convergence lab: running mean (LLN), histogram of batch estimates against the Gaussian the CLT predicts, log-log SE vs N. Retire `quant_builds/monte_carlo/engine.py` with a pointer.
- *Key concept:* The estimator is itself a random variable; a Monte Carlo price without an error bar is meaningless; coverage is the operational meaning of a confidence interval.
- *Validation:* The seeded CI test checks the interval brackets the analytic Black-Scholes price; the full coverage study — 200 independently-seeded pricings, ~95% bracketing within binomial tolerance — runs *locally* via a make target with results committed as a figure and table (see Section 5 for why it must not block CI); fitted SE-vs-N slope = −0.5; digital option matches its closed form e^(−rT)·N(d2).

**Milestone 5 — Martingale checks and the variance-reduction tournament (Weeks 7–8)**
- *Build:* (a) Martingale property on simulated paths: e^(−rt)·S_t is a martingale under Q (drift r) and demonstrably not under P (drift μ). (b) Rebuild antithetic and control variates as composable estimator decorators, add stratified sampling, and price an arithmetic-average Asian option using the geometric-Asian closed form (which you implement — a new oracle) as control variate. Produce the tournament table: variance per unit compute for each technique. This is what distinguishes the new library from your old build: the same tricks, now applied to a payoff with *no* closed form, with measurement discipline.
- *Key concept:* Each variance-reduction method is a probability lesson in costume — antithetics = covariance and monotonicity, control variates = conditional expectation and regression, stratification = law of total variance (Glasserman Ch. 4).
- *Validation:* Martingale test passes under Q, fails under P with the predicted drift gap; geometric-Asian MC brackets its own closed form; measured variance reduction from the control (target ~90%+) matches the 1 − ρ² prediction from the estimated correlation, and the fitted control coefficient matches β = Cov/Var; all estimators agree within joint error bars.

**Milestone 6 — Girsanov made visible (Week 9)**
- *Build:* `measure.py`: price the same European option (a) simulating under Q directly and (b) simulating under P with drift μ, weighting each payoff by the pathwise Radon-Nikodym derivative exp(−θW_T − θ²T/2), θ = (μ−r)/σ. Then invert the trick: for a deep OTM option, shift the drift toward the strike and reweight — importance sampling, which *is* Girsanov. Add the negative result: sweep |μ − r| and plot how the reweighted estimator's variance grows — the practical cost of a far-away measure, and the reason the weight tests below can degrade.
- *Key concept:* Change of measure reweights the probabilities of paths, not the paths; this is why drift never appears in option prices. Lead with Lalley's lecture notes and the experiment itself; treat Shreve Vol II Ch. 5 as reference, not prerequisite — it is above your current level and the experiment is how you grow into it.
- *Validation:* Both estimators' CIs overlap each other and bracket Black-Scholes; the sample mean of the RN weights is ~1 within SE at moderate θ (it is a P-martingale with expectation 1), with the documented heavy-tailed degradation at large θ; importance sampling on the OTM option delivers a measured SE reduction factor at fixed N — report what you measure, not what the literature promises.

### Phase B — extension track, in priority order (weeks 10–18)

**Milestone 7 — Greeks three ways (Weeks 10–11)**
- *Build:* `greeks.py`: delta and vega by (a) bump-and-reprice with common random numbers, (b) the pathwise derivative method, (c) the likelihood-ratio method, following Haugh's Columbia notes and Glasserman Ch. 7. Bias/variance/cost comparison table against the Milestone-1 analytic oracle, including the digital option.
- *Key concept:* Interchanging differentiation and expectation — when it is legal (pathwise) and how to dodge it by differentiating the density instead (likelihood ratio); estimator bias-variance tradeoffs.
- *Validation:* All three agree with analytic Greeks within error bars on the vanilla call; the bump-size study shows bias growing with h and variance exploding as h→0 without common random numbers — plot it; pathwise delta demonstrably fails on the digital (discontinuous payoff) while likelihood-ratio matches the closed-form digital delta — a documented negative result.

**Milestone 8 — The delta-hedging P&L lab (Weeks 12–13)**
- *Build:* `hedging.py`: sell a call at the Black-Scholes price and delta-hedge along thousands of GBM paths with full self-financing portfolio accounting (stock position, cash accruing at r), rebalancing frequency configurable from weekly to several times daily. Second experiment: hedge with the *wrong* volatility and decompose P&L into the gamma-weighted realized-minus-implied variance term.
- *Key concept:* The deepest "why" of Black-Scholes: the price is the cost of replication; volatility, not direction, is the traded quantity. This is the most interview-defensible experiment in the repo.
- *Validation:* The self-financing invariant (cash + stock − option = portfolio value) asserted at *every* timestep; mean hedging P&L statistically zero at every frequency; P&L standard deviation shrinks ~1/√(rebalances), fitted on log-log axes; the zero-rebalancing limit reproduces the unhedged payoff variance (a free sanity anchor); hedging at implied vol above realized vol yields systematically positive P&L for the short-option seller, sign flipping with the inequality, magnitude consistent with the ½·Γ·S²·(σ²_real − σ²_imp)·dt accumulation.

**Milestone 9 — Stopping times and American exercise (Weeks 14–16)**
- *Build:* (a) A one-week stopping-time lab: simulate asymmetric gambler's ruin, estimate ruin probability and duration by Monte Carlo against the closed-form (q/p)^k answer, then construct the exponential martingale (q/p)^(S_n), stop it at the exit time, and check E[M_τ] = M_0 empirically — the optional stopping theorem, verified. (b) Add early exercise to the Milestone-2 tree (max(intrinsic, continuation)) and plot the exercise boundary. (c) Longstaff-Schwartz LSMC on the path engine: polynomial regression of continuation value on ITM paths only; the regression decides *when* to exercise, realized cash flows determine the *value*.
- *Key concept:* Stopping times and optional stopping — martingale theory doing real work; conditional expectation estimated by regression; a data-leakage lesson (foresight bias) that transfers directly to backtesting.
- *Validation:* Ruin probability inside its CLT interval around the closed form; E[M_τ] = M_0 within SE; American call on a non-dividend stock exactly equals the European (the no-early-exercise theorem, asserted); American put ≥ European everywhere, boundary monotone; LSMC put within cents of a 2000-step tree, at or slightly below it (the known low bias) and above the European price; **foresight bias as a deliberate failing test** — implement the wrong version that uses the regression's fitted value as the value, and assert the price shifts high. Keep it as a negative regression test.

**Milestone 10 — Capstone: a real implied-vol smile, plus the report (Weeks 17–18)**
- *Build:* Point the library at reality. On day one of the milestone, pull an option chain via yfinance (loop over expirations) and **immediately cache the raw pull to disk** — all cleaning code runs against the cached file, so a flaky unofficial API cannot block you. Use SPY (or ^SPX, which is genuinely European-style) and stick to near-ATM strikes and short-to-mid tenors, where the early-exercise premium your European solver ignores is smallest; avoid deep-ITM puts and long expiries where it is material. Clean: mid = (bid+ask)/2 (never lastPrice), drop zero-bid/zero-open-interest strikes, cap spreads. Pull the tenor-matched Treasury rate from FRED (sign up for the free API key back in week 1) and the trailing dividend yield from yfinance. Compute IVs with *your own* Newton-with-vega solver plus a Brent/bisection fallback — never Yahoo's black-box column — and plot per-expiry smiles, upgrading your existing `iv_surface.py` visualization. Run butterfly and calendar checks in *forensic mode*: on raw retail quotes some will fail — delayed, unsynchronized, American-style data guarantees it — and documenting the violations and their causes is the deliverable, not asserting a pass. Plot your IVs against Yahoo's impliedVolatility column and explain the discrepancies via rate/dividend/exercise-style assumptions — differences here are input forensics, not bugs. **Fallbacks are legitimate completions:** if yfinance breaks mid-capstone, use the free DoltHub `post-no-preference/options` historical database, or a synthetic chain generated from a known smile. Finish with the technical report: a theorem → experiment → test map of the whole library, an architecture diagram, the validation battery as a table, and an honest limitations section — regenerable end-to-end from one `make`/`nox` command with fixed seeds. Schedule the write-up; it is half the portfolio value.
- *Key concept:* The smile as the market's correction to lognormality; IV as a model output; convexity of call prices in strike as positivity of the risk-neutral density (Breeden-Litzenberger).
- *Validation:* Round-trip price → your IV → reprice recovers inputs to ~1e-8 on synthetic data; your solver matches py_vollib ("Let's Be Rational") to ~1e-6 on clean quotes, with the deep-ITM/stale-quote failures of naive Newton documented; post-cleaning monotonicity/convexity checks with residual violations explained; the entire pytest suite — nine milestones of encoded theorems — green in CI.

**Flex module — barrier options and first passage (2 weeks, unscheduled).** Insert after Milestone 7 only if you are on or ahead of schedule; it is the first thing to cut. Validate: bias vs number of monitoring dates shrinks like √dt; the corrected estimator lands within CI of the continuous-barrier (Merton/Reiner-Rubinstein) closed form; knock-in + knock-out = vanilla within error bars.

## 5. Validation discipline

These standing rules are what make the repo a research artifact instead of a toy. Write them into `docs/TESTING.md` in week 1 and treat them as constitutional.

1. **Notebooks call, never define.** All pricing logic lives in tested modules; notebooks are consumers. This is the anti-relapse rule.
2. **Every stochastic tolerance is CLT-derived.** Assertions on random output use k standard errors, never magic numbers like `abs(mc − bs) < 0.01`. A correct 95% test fails ~5% of the time by design — your tolerances must account for that or your CI will punish correct code.
3. **Two-tier testing resolves the seeding tension.** CI runs the deterministic, fixed-seed suite (meaningful green badge, no flakes). The genuinely randomized studies — 200-run CI coverage, convergence-slope refits — run locally via a make target, with the resulting figures and tables committed. A seeded coverage test is a vacuous re-run; an unseeded one flakes in CI; this split is the honest resolution.
4. **Bitwise reproducibility.** Identical seeds produce identical paths, asserted in a test; every figure in the report regenerates from one command.
5. **The theorem harness.** The permanent battery any new pricer must pass: put-call parity to 1e-12; agreement with the analytic oracle, py_vollib, and Hull fixtures; tree-to-BS convergence; MC confidence intervals bracketing closed forms with SE ~ 1/√N; cross-method Greek agreement; martingale and RN-weight checks; LSMC at-or-below a fine tree and above the European price; hedging P&L mean ~0 with variance shrinking in rebalance frequency. Derivatives code has no ground truth to eyeball — it is validated by internal consistency, and that meta-lesson is the point.
6. **Three error types, named in every debugging session:** statistical error (paths), discretization error (steps), and bugs. Milestone 3 exists to separate them; conflating them makes the back half hopeless.
7. **Negative results are deliverables.** Pathwise Greeks failing on digitals, the foresight-bias test, RN-weight variance growing with |μ − r|, arbitrage violations on retail quotes — documented failures you can explain are more impressive than suspicious perfection.
8. **Honest numbers only.** Measured variance-reduction factors, fitted slopes, cent-level LSMC agreement. No fantasy metrics anywhere; the strongest claim this repo makes is "every theorem in this library is enforced by a test," and that beats any Sharpe ratio.

## 6. Data & tools

**Phase A and most of Phase B: zero market data.** This is deliberate — pure simulation means no data-quality confounders while you learn the probability, and a part-time student is never blocked on plumbing. Stack: NumPy/SciPy/Matplotlib (which you know), plus pytest, ruff, and GitHub Actions (which you are learning). Free pip-installable oracles: py_vollib (Jaeckel's "Let's Be Rational" — note the package naming churn back toward `vollib` from v1.0.7) and optionally QuantLib-Python as a second oracle; Hull's worked examples as fixture values.

**Milestone 10 only:**
- **yfinance** `Ticker.option_chain()` — free, but unofficial, rate-limited, snapshot-only (no history), no Greeks, and it has broken repeatedly after Yahoo API changes. Loop expirations yourself; *cache every raw pull immediately*; expect stale `lastPrice` values and zero-bid illiquid strikes — cleaning them is course content, not a chore. Never trust the precomputed `impliedVolatility` column: it embeds undisclosed rate/dividend assumptions.
- **FRED via fredapi** — free API key (sign up in week 1, not week 17); pull the Treasury tenor matched to each option expiry instead of hardcoding r = 0.05.
- **Dividend yield from yfinance** — trailing, not implied; your IVs will therefore still disagree with market convention, and that framing belongs in the write-up so discrepancy is not mistaken for bug.
- **DoltHub `post-no-preference/options`** — the only real free source of *historical* end-of-day chains (~2019-present, plain SQL); your fallback if Yahoo breaks, and the gateway to any stretch-goal backtest.
- Total data spend: zero dollars. Everything runs on a laptop.

## 8. Stretch directions

Once the ladder is green, each of these extends the same architecture without a rewrite — that extensibility is itself the proof your design worked:

- **Heston integration:** fold your existing `quant_builds/heston` into the PathGenerator interface (full-truncation Euler), price vanillas, validate against the semi-analytic characteristic-function price, then invert Heston-simulated prices through your own solver to reproduce a smile — tying two old builds into the new library.
- **SVI and calibration:** fit Gatheral's SVI slices to your capstone smiles with butterfly/calendar no-arbitrage encoded as tests; then calibrate Heston to the surface — your introduction to inverse problems, parameter identifiability, and model risk, the closest a self-learner gets to desk-quant work.
- **The full arbitrage-checked 3D surface** (promoted from the capstone's forensic mode), with a Breeden-Litzenberger risk-neutral density extraction.
- **QuantLib reconciliation report:** cross-validate the whole library against QuantLib-Python and document every discrepancy and its cause — the most desk-realistic exercise available to a student.
- **Performance track:** quasi-Monte Carlo via Sobol sequences (scipy.stats.qmc) with the improved convergence rate measured; then port the hot path-generation loop to Numba or C++/pybind11 with the Python tests as the correctness oracle — the classic QuantStart-style bridge toward production pricing code.
- **Ship it:** publish to TestPyPI with versioned releases and an mkdocs documentation site, completing the software-engineering arc; optionally a small Streamlit front end (price, Greeks, hedging P&L histograms) as the interview demo layer.
- **The report as writing sample:** the theorem → experiment → test document, polished, doubles as a financial-engineering grad-application artifact.
